# Midterm Project Report - 10%

The midterm report is a major project checkpoint. By this point, the team must have progressed beyond planning and must provide concrete evidence of implementation and experimentation.

At minimum, the team should have prepared the data, implemented a working experimental pipeline, and evaluated at least one meaningful baseline or model.

The midterm report should update and extend the proposal rather than repeat it without revision.

Research projects may change as the team learns more about the data and problem. Any important change from the proposal must be clearly explained and justified.

## Midterm Sections and Checklist

### 1. Updated Introduction, Background, and Problem Definition

Revise the introduction, prior-work discussion, problem definition, and motivation based on what the team has learned since the proposal.

Address:

* Whether the original problem remains feasible.
* Whether the technical question has changed.
* Whether the intended use or project scope has changed.
* Whether new prior work has influenced the project.
* Whether the team's original assumptions remain valid.

`✅ Updated Project Context`

`✅ Updated Problem Definition`

`✅ Updated and Verified Prior Work`

`✅ Important Changes Clearly Explained`

Do not leave outdated proposal statements in the report when the project has changed.

### 2. Data Status and Analysis

Describe the current status of the data.

Include:

* The final or current data source.
* Data-access status.
* Data size and important variables.
* Cleaning, filtering, labeling, sampling, or preprocessing already completed.
* Exploratory analysis and useful visualizations.
* Data-quality problems.
* Leakage, imbalance, bias, privacy, or licensing concerns.
* Changes from the proposal.

`✅ Data Successfully Accessed`

`✅ Data Preparation Documented`

`✅ Exploratory Analysis Included`

`✅ Data Limitations Identified`

`✅ Privacy and Licensing Rechecked`

The report should present evidence that the team can actually use the data, not merely a plan to obtain it later.

### 3. Implemented Methods

Describe the methods that have been implemented.

At minimum, include:

* A working baseline or comparison method.
* The current preprocessing pipeline.
* At least one implemented ML, AI, or deep-learning method.
* Important model settings or hyperparameters.
* A justification for the implemented methods.
* Relevant implementation or engineering decisions.

`✅ Working Experimental Pipeline`

`✅ Meaningful Baseline Implemented`

`✅ At Least One Model or Technical Method Implemented`

`✅ Method Selection Explained`

`✅ Important Hyperparameters Documented`

The report must distinguish between work that has actually been implemented and work that is still planned.

### 4. Preliminary Results and Discussion

Present quantitative and, when appropriate, qualitative results.

Include:

* Appropriate evaluation metrics.
* At least one useful visualization.
* Baseline or model performance.
* Interpretation of the results.
* Important errors or failure cases.
* Evidence of overfitting, underfitting, leakage, instability, bias, or other concerns when applicable.
* What the results suggest about the project's main question.

`✅ Quantitative Results`

`✅ Meaningful Visualizations`

`✅ Baseline or Model Evaluation`

`✅ Interpretation of Results`

`✅ Error or Failure Analysis`

`✅ Evidence Distinguished from Speculation`

Do not present metric values without explaining what they mean and why they matter.

### 5. Remaining Work and Revised Plan

Explain what remains to be completed before the final submission.

Include:

* Planned additional models, variants, ablations, or experiments.
* Important technical blockers.
* Planned improvements to data or evaluation.
* Planned interactive system, visualization, deployment, or public-facing artifact when applicable.
* Revised team responsibilities.
* Updated milestones and schedule.
* Major risks and contingency plans.

`✅ Clear Next Steps`

`✅ Updated Team Responsibilities`

`✅ Revised Gantt Chart or Milestone Plan`

`✅ Risks and Contingency Plans`

The remaining plan should be realistic for the time left in the semester.

### 6. References, Citation Verification, and Generative-AI Disclosure

Update the references and Citation Verification Table.

Every new citation must:

* Exist.
* Be opened and reviewed by at least one team member.
* Support the claim for which it is cited.
* Include accurate bibliographic information.

Also update the team's generative-AI disclosure to describe:

* How generative AI has been used.
* What AI-generated output was accepted, modified, or rejected.
* How generated code, claims, and references were verified.
* Any privacy, licensing, or security concerns.

`✅ Updated References`

`✅ Updated Citation Verification Table`

`✅ Every New Citation Verified`

`✅ Updated Generative-AI Disclosure`

The assigned TA may ask any team member about any cited paper or source during the required TA Progress Meeting.

## Midterm Submission Requirements

### 1. Updated Project Website and Midterm Report

The midterm report must be published on the team's project website and accessible to the instructional team.

There is no fixed word limit. However, the report should be focused, well organized, and technically complete.

Reuse and update the proposal page rather than creating disconnected or contradictory versions of the project.

`✅ Updated Website Accessible`

`✅ All Midterm Sections Included`

`✅ Legible Figures and Tables`

`✅ Important Proposal Changes Identified`

### 2. Evidence of Implementation

The submission must include concrete evidence that the project has moved beyond planning.

Acceptable evidence may include:

* Baseline results.
* Model outputs.
* Evaluation tables.
* Visualizations.
* Example predictions.
* Training curves.
* Error-analysis examples.
* A working preliminary interface or system.
* Other verifiable technical artifacts.

`✅ Working Baseline or Model`

`✅ Quantitative or Qualitative Evidence`

`✅ Preliminary Analysis`

Screenshots alone are not a substitute for accessible code and reproducible results.

### 3. Repository and README

Reuse the private repository created for the proposal.

The repository must include:

* Relevant source code.
* Data-processing scripts or instructions.
* Configuration files when applicable.
* Current experiment or evaluation code.
* A clear `README.md`.
* Instructions for reproducing the current results.
* A description of important files and directories.
* Dependency or environment information.

The README may use the following format:

`/directory/`: Description of the directory

`/directory/file.py`: Description of the file

`✅ Repository Updated`

`✅ README Updated`

`✅ Current Code Accessible`

`✅ Preliminary Reproduction Instructions`

Large, protected, or licensed data should not be uploaded when doing so would violate privacy, ethics, terms, or licensing requirements. Provide approved access or reconstruction instructions instead.

### 4. Updated Planning and Contribution Materials

Include:

* Updated Gantt chart or milestone plan.
* Updated team responsibilities.
* Midterm contribution table.
* Updated Citation Verification Table.
* Updated generative-AI disclosure.

The midterm contribution table should identify each team member's specific work since the proposal.

`✅ Updated Gantt Chart`

`✅ Midterm Contribution Table`

`✅ Citation Verification Table`

`✅ Generative-AI Disclosure`

### 5. Canvas Submission

Submit the required links and materials through the designated Canvas assignment.

The submission should include:

* Midterm report or project website link.
* Repository link.
* Required tables and disclosures.
* Any additional item listed in Canvas.

All links must be accessible to the instructional team before the deadline.

## Relationship to the TA Progress Meeting

The Required TA Progress Meeting is a separate project component worth 5% of the course grade.

It occurs after the proposal and before the midterm report. The meeting provides feedback and verifies individual understanding, but it does not replace any midterm-report requirement.

Teams are expected to address important corrections, concerns, or action items identified during the meeting.

## Midterm Report Rubric - 10 Course Points

| Criterion                                                                          |   Points |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------: |
| Updated problem definition, background, and verified prior work                    |      1.5 |
| Data preparation, exploratory analysis, and responsible data handling              |      1.5 |
| Working baseline, implemented method, and technical justification                  |      2.0 |
| Preliminary results, visualizations, evaluation, and interpretation                |      2.5 |
| Error analysis, limitations, remaining work, and realistic revised plan            |      1.5 |
| Reproducibility, contribution records, citation verification, and GenAI disclosure |      1.0 |
| **Total**                                                                          | **10.0** |
