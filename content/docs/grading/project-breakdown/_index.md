# Individual and Team Projects

There are **two separate projects**. The individual project contributes 30% and the team project 40% of the course grade; see [Grading Categories](../categories/). Keep one individual website link and one different team website link for the whole semester, improving each site at its scheduled checkpoints. Register and maintain the links in [ML Class Hub](https://ml-class.cc.gatech.edu/). The [course schedule](../../course-info/course-schedule-mahdi/) gives the dates.

## Part 1: Individual project

Follow the [Individual Project requirements](individual/) for the assigned dataset and analysis sequence. The midterm is an unsupervised-learning website update plus one 10-minute unlisted YouTube video; the final adds supervised comparisons, a completed website, and a TA interview. The course-specific Hub assignment will identify the dataset, task labels, and checkpoint details once staff configure it. Your individual project must demonstrate your own understanding.

## Part 2: Team project

Form a registered team, choose a feasible and meaningful ML question, and build one team website. The stages are the [team proposal](proposal/), [team midterm website review and TA meeting](midterm/), and [final report, website, and TA interview](final/). All members must understand the entire project, not only their assigned tasks. The proposal, review, and final pages explain what to show at each stage; there is no separate team midterm report or video.

## Choose an ambitious but feasible topic

A strong topic has a clear question, data you can lawfully use, a baseline, an evaluation plan, and time for error analysis. Possible domains include language, vision, health or scientific data, recommendations, time series, security, robotics, and responsible AI. Simply calling a model API, using a fashionable dataset, or promising high accuracy is not enough. Ask what your project will teach someone that a straightforward baseline cannot.

Originality can come from a new question, data source, application setting, careful comparison, method adaptation, evaluation under realistic constraints, or a usable system. Novelty is encouraged, **not required for full credit**. A careful, reproducible study can be excellent even when its models are established. The [past awardees](award_galore/) offer inspiration, not templates to copy; some older Georgia Tech links require sign-in.

Treat the project as portfolio-quality work: document your own contribution, methods, evidence, limitations, and code so you can explain it on a résumé or in an interview. A project can grow into later research or a paper, but publication is not promised by this course and requires additional work, appropriate review, and any applicable data or human-subject approvals.

## Shared integrity and data rules

Both projects permit disclosed, verified generative-AI assistance under [General Course Policies](../../guidelines/general/). Cite sources you actually read; verify claims, results, and references. Protect private and licensed data. Describe individual contributions honestly. The [award and recognition criteria](award_galore/) reward work beyond a polished appearance.
