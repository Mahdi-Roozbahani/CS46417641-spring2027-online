# Semester Project Breakdown

The semester project is the primary applied and creative component of the course. It is worth **55% of the required course grade**, with an additional **optional bonus of up to +5 course percentage points**.

The maximum possible overall course score is **105%**.

## Important Project Rules

* The required project is worth **55%** of the course grade.
* Students may earn up to **+5 additional course percentage points** through the optional project bonus.
* The normal team size is exactly five students.
* Each team has exactly two required TA interactions.
* Every student must understand the entire project, not only the part they personally completed.
* Each student will be asked individual questions during both required TA interactions.
* Individual Q&A is graded separately. A student who cannot answer questions about the project may receive a lower individual project grade than the rest of the team.
* Students may be asked questions about any part of the project, including methods, code, results, datasets, and cited papers.
* Fake, fabricated, or misleading citations are prohibited and may be referred to the Georgia Tech Office of Student Integrity.
* Generative AI is allowed for the project, but all use must be disclosed and verified.

## Open Project Scope

There are no project tracks. Teams may propose any legitimate project involving machine learning, artificial intelligence, or deep learning, including but not limited to:

* Tabular prediction
* Natural-language processing
* Computer vision
* Generative AI
* Recommendation and ranking
* Time series
* Reinforcement learning
* Robotics and sensing
* Scientific or engineering machine learning
* Responsible, secure, interpretable, or robust AI

A topic is not approved merely because it uses an AI API. The project must contain a meaningful technical question, data or an evaluation setting, appropriate baselines, experiments, analysis, and conclusions.

## Team Composition

* Every team should have exactly five registered students.
* Teams of four or six may be permitted only when necessary because of enrollment or team-formation constraints.
* Students may not create a team of four or six without approval from the instructional team.
* A student may belong to only one project team.
* Students who do not join a complete team by the deadline may be assigned to or merged with another team.
* Team membership is normally locked after the proposal deadline.

Students may use the designated Ed Discussion threads to find teammates. Students should not publicly post sensitive personal information.

## Required Project Components: 55%

| Component                                                | Team portion | Individual portion | Total course weight |
| -------------------------------------------------------- | -----------: | -----------------: | ------------------: |
| Proposal and team charter                                |           5% |                 0% |                  5% |
| Required TA Progress Meeting                             |           3% |                 2% |                  5% |
| Midterm project report                                   |          10% |                 0% |                 10% |
| Final website, report, code, and reproducibility package |          25% |                 0% |                 25% |
| Final presentation and individual Q&A                    |           6% |                 4% |                 10% |
| **Required project total**                               |      **49%** |             **6%** |             **55%** |

Most project components receive a common team grade. However, the individual Q&A portions are graded separately for each student.

## Exactly Two Required TA Interactions

Each team will be assigned a project TA for continuity. The team will have exactly two required TA interactions:

1. **TA Progress Meeting:** Held after the proposal and before the midterm report.
2. **Final Presentation:** Held near the end of the semester.

Students may also ask project questions during regular office hours or through Ed Discussion. These optional interactions do not create additional required project meetings.

## Individual Project-Wide Q&A

During both required TA interactions, the TA will ask questions directly to individual team members.

The TA may ask any student about any part of the project, including:

* Motivation and problem definition
* Data source, preprocessing, and limitations
* Prior work and cited papers
* Baselines and models
* Training and evaluation design
* Metrics and statistical interpretation
* Results and failure cases
* Code and reproducibility
* Responsible-AI considerations
* Generative-AI use

**The TA may also select a cited paper or source and ask any team member to explain:**

* The main contribution of the source
* How the source relates to the project
* Which claim or design decision it supports
* Whether the team reproduced, modified, or disagreed with its approach

A student may not answer, “That was another team member’s part,” as a substitute for understanding.

A student who cannot reasonably explain the project or answer the TA’s questions will lose points from the individual Q&A portion. This reduction applies to that student and does not automatically reduce the grades of the other team members.

## Forty-Eight-Hour Follow-Up Video

When a student gives an incomplete or incorrect answer during a required TA interaction, the TA may permit one recorded follow-up response.

The follow-up is not automatic. It is offered at the TA’s discretion and must be submitted within **48 hours of the original meeting or presentation**.

The TA will identify the questions or topics that the student must address. The video must:

* Show the student on camera.
* Be recorded specifically for this purpose.
* Answer the questions assigned by the TA.
* Explain the response in the student’s own words.
* Demonstrate understanding rather than merely reading prepared text.
* Be submitted privately using the method specified by the TA.

A follow-up video may recover up to **50% of the individual Q&A points originally lost**. It cannot normally replace the complete live Q&A score.

Failure to submit the video within 48 hours means that the original individual Q&A score remains unchanged.

## Absence from the Final Presentation

Every team member is expected to attend and participate in the scheduled final presentation.

A student who cannot attend because of an officially documented exceptional circumstance must:

1. Follow the official Georgia Tech exceptional-circumstances process.
2. Notify the assigned TA through a private Ed Discussion post as soon as reasonably possible.
3. Submit an individual replacement video within **48 hours of the scheduled team presentation**, unless the instructional team approves a different deadline.

The replacement video must include:

* A clear overview of the complete project.
* The project motivation and technical question.
* The data and preprocessing process.
* The models and baselines.
* The evaluation design and metrics.
* The main results and failure cases.
* The project limitations.
* The student’s individual contributions.
* Answers to any additional questions assigned by the TA.

A student with an officially approved absence may earn the full individual presentation and Q&A points through the replacement process.

An unapproved absence does not automatically qualify for a replacement presentation. A student who misses the presentation without an approved exception may receive zero for the individual presentation and Q&A portion.

## Whole-Project Understanding

Every team member must understand the complete project.

Dividing work among team members is allowed and expected, but task division does not remove the responsibility to understand:

* What the team attempted
* Why the team selected its methods
* How the code and experiments work
* What the results mean
* Where the project failed
* Which external sources influenced the work
* What each team member contributed

The purpose of the individual questions is to verify that every student participated meaningfully and understands the work submitted under the team’s name.

## Generative AI Is Allowed on the Project

Teams may use generative AI for brainstorming, coding, debugging, analysis, writing support, visualization, or other project tasks.

All generative-AI use must comply with the project disclosure, verification, privacy, licensing, and citation requirements.

The team remains fully responsible for:

* Correctness of code
* Validity of experiments
* Accuracy of claims
* Authenticity of references
* Reproducibility of reported results
* Proper attribution
* Compliance with data and software licenses

“The AI produced it” is not a defense for an error, false claim, fake citation, or academic-integrity violation.

## Citation Verification Requirement

Every cited source must:

* Actually exist.
* Be opened and reviewed by at least one team member.
* Be cited accurately.
* Support the statement for which it is cited.
* Include accurate authors, title, venue, year, URL, DOI, or other identifying information.

Teams must maintain the required Citation Verification Table.

**Fake or fabricated citations are prohibited.**

This includes:

* Invented papers, titles, authors, or venues
* Fabricated dates, quotations, URLs, or DOIs
* References generated by AI that the team did not verify
* Citations to a real paper for a claim that the paper does not support
* Listing a source that no team member has opened or reviewed

The teaching team may spot-check references at any stage. During either required TA interaction, any student may be asked to explain a cited source and how it informed the project.

Suspected fabricated or intentionally misleading citations may be referred to the Georgia Tech Office of Student Integrity.

## Team Charter and Conflict Prevention

The proposal must include a team charter defining:

* Expected weekly contribution
* Recurring meeting schedule
* Primary communication channel
* Expected response time
* Initial roles and responsibilities
* How roles may rotate
* Repository and document access
* Decision-making process
* Procedure for missed commitments
* Procedure for internal disagreements

## Conflict Escalation

1. The team should first discuss the specific problem and refer to its team charter.
2. If the problem remains unresolved, the affected student should submit a private Ed Discussion post containing relevant facts, dates, evidence, and prior attempts to resolve the issue.
3. The assigned project TA may conduct a short mediation meeting and establish an action plan.
4. If the action plan fails or the matter is serious, the TA may escalate the issue to a Head TA or the instructor.

Students should not wait until the final week to report a semester-long participation problem.

Concerns involving safety, harassment, discrimination, retaliation, threats, or academic misconduct may be escalated immediately.

## Peer and Contribution Evidence

The teaching team may use the following information when evaluating individual participation:

* Confidential peer feedback
* Git history
* Document revision history
* Task records
* Meeting attendance
* Contribution tables
* Individual Q&A performance
* TA observations
* Evidence submitted during a conflict review

The normal team score may be adjusted for an individual student when there is clear and documented evidence of substantially different contribution, participation, or understanding.

## Data, Privacy, and Licensing

Teams must verify that they are permitted to access, process, store, and publish the data used in the project.

* Do not place protected, regulated, confidential, private, or personally identifiable data in a public repository or generative-AI tool.
* Follow dataset licenses, API terms, website terms, and software licenses.
* Cite every external dataset and document its version or access date.
* Explain data collection, filtering, labeling, exclusions, and known biases.
* Projects involving human-subject research or protected data may require approvals that are not feasible within the course timeline.

## Required Public-Facing Artifact

The final project must be presented through a clear project website, normally using GitHub Pages, Streamlit, or another approved platform.

The website should clearly communicate:

* The project problem
* Motivation
* Data
* Methods
* Baselines
* Experimental evidence
* Results
* Failure cases
* Limitations
* References
* Team contributions

Code or data that cannot legally or ethically be made public must still be accessible to the instructional team through an approved private repository or submission method.
