# General Course Policies

## Online and Asynchronous Participation

All core lectures are pre-recorded. There is no required live lecture attendance. Students are responsible for watching the assigned videos, studying the lecture notes, completing assessments within their availability windows, and participating in the team project.

Because this is an asynchronous course with students in many time zones, routine deadlines use Anywhere on Earth so every student can use the full listed calendar date.

## Official Platforms and Communication

### Ed Discussion

Ed Discussion is the official location for announcements, class-wide clarifications, question-and-answer threads, office-hour notices, and private messages to the teaching team. Students must enable notifications and check Ed regularly.

### Canvas

Canvas is the official location for lecture modules, quizzes, exams, project submissions, approved reference-sheet uploads, and the gradebook. Canvas timestamps determine whether a submission was received before a deadline.

### Gradescope

Gradescope is used only for the ungraded problem sets. A displayed score or automated result on Gradescope is diagnostic feedback and does not contribute to the course grade.

## Anywhere on Earth Deadlines

A due date means 23:59 Anywhere on Earth, abbreviated AoE, unless an official Ed Discussion announcement states a course-wide exception. AoE is UTC-12. It allows every student, regardless of location, to use the full listed calendar date. Canvas may display the equivalent deadline in the student's local time zone.

Students should convert AoE to their local time well before a deadline. Time-zone misunderstanding, daylight-saving changes, travel, work schedules, or an incorrect personal-calendar conversion do not create an automatic extension.

## Assessment Independence

Quizzes and exams must be completed independently. Students may not communicate with another person, use generative AI, search the web, consult unauthorized notes, use a second device, or obtain assistance during a quiz or exam.

The project has different rules. Collaboration within the registered team and disclosed use of generative AI are allowed, subject to the project requirements.

## Course Materials and Problem Sets

Lecture videos, lecture notes, quiz questions, exam questions, problem sets, and course-created code are provided for enrolled students. Note that the problem sets (homeworks) solutions will not be avaialble and students are responsible to use Gradescope auto grader and benefit from ML instructional team members to solve them and understand them. Students may not post, sell, upload, transmit, or redistribute these materials outside the course without written permission.

The four problem sets are optional and ungraded. Students may discuss concepts from them on Ed Discussion at the whiteboard level, but they may not publicly post complete course solutions or distribute official solution materials.

## Generative AI Policy by Activity

| Activity | Generative AI rule |
|---|---|
| Quiz 0, graded quizzes, midterm, final | Prohibited |
| Ungraded problem sets | Allowed for learning after making a genuine independent attempt; not allowed to obtain or distribute official course solutions |
| Semester project | Allowed with complete disclosure, verification, and compliance with privacy, licensing, citation, and academic-integrity rules |

For the project, students must disclose the tool, purpose, and scope of use. Students must verify AI-generated code, text, data, results, and references. Students may not enter sensitive, protected, regulated, confidential, licensed, or personally identifiable information into an AI system.

## Citation and Reference Integrity

Every citation in a project submission must correspond to a real source that a team member has opened and verified. The source must support the claim for which it is cited.

The following are prohibited:

- Fabricated or nonexistent papers, books, websites, datasets, authors, venues, years, quotations, DOIs, or URLs.
- Citing a paper based only on an AI-generated summary without verifying the original source.
- Listing a source in the references that is never cited in the report.
- Using an in-text citation that has no matching reference entry.
- Citing a source that does not support the associated claim.
- Copying language, figures, tables, code, or ideas without appropriate attribution.

**Fabricated citations and intentionally false source information are academic-integrity violations. Suspected cases may be referred to the Georgia Tech Office of Student Integrity.**

## Academic Integrity and OSI

The Georgia Tech Academic Honor Code applies to all course activities. Suspected violations will be handled according to Georgia Tech procedures. Course staff may preserve relevant Canvas, Honorlock, Ed, Gradescope, Git, document-history, and submission records.

A referral is not a determination of responsibility. The Office of Student Integrity determines responsibility and sanctions under Institute policy.

## Regrade and Grade-Correction Requests

- Follow the regrade instructions and deadline stated with the assessment.
- Identify a specific grading or answer-key issue and explain the requested correction.
- A regrade may result in a score increasing, remaining unchanged, or decreasing if the entire response is reviewed.
- Problem-set results are ungraded and are not eligible for a course-grade regrade.
- General requests for additional points, rounding, or exceptions without a specific error will not be considered.

## Technical Problems During Assessments

Students should use Quiz 0 to test their computer, camera, microphone, browser, network, and Honorlock setup. If a technical problem occurs during a proctored assessment:

1. Use Honorlock live chat immediately.
2. Follow the proctor's instructions.
3. Record the support case number or take a permitted screenshot after the assessment if possible.
4. Submit a private Ed post promptly with the assessment name, time, support documentation, and a factual description.
5. Do not attempt to bypass Honorlock or continue through an unauthorized method.

A technical issue does not automatically produce a retake. The teaching team will review the available records and Institute policies.

## Exceptional Circumstances

Any request for an exception to course policies or deadlines should be made in advance whenever reasonably possible. Requests should be limited to incapacitating illness, hospitalization, serious personal or family emergencies, or similarly exceptional circumstances.

Your request **MUST** be supported by an official letter or notification from the [Georgia Tech Office of the Dean of Students](https://studentlife.gatech.edu/request-assistance).

Before contacting the ML instructional team, you must first submit a request through the Dean of Students Request Assistance page.

After submitting your request to the Dean of Students, notify the ML instructional team through a **private Ed Discussion post**. In the private post, confirm that you have contacted the Dean of Students and identify the affected quiz, examination, project deliverable, or other course deadline.

The instructor and teaching assistants cannot independently evaluate personal circumstances, medical information, or supporting documentation. Do not send medical records, hospital documents, diagnostic information, or other sensitive personal information directly to the instructor, teaching assistants, or through Ed Discussion.

Submitting a private Ed Discussion post does not, by itself, approve an exception. The instructional team can consider the request only after receiving the appropriate supporting letter or official notification from the Dean of Students.

**The supporting letter or official notification from the Dean of Students must be received within one week of the affected due date for the instructional team to consider an accommodation. If the assignment, quiz, examination, project deliverable, or other requirement was due more than one week earlier, we will not be able to provide a retroactive accommodation.**

Job interviews, ordinary work conflicts, personal travel, vacations, conferences, time-zone mistakes, forgotten deadlines, and competing academic or professional obligations do NOT qualify as exceptional circumstances.


## Accommodations for Students with Disabilities

Students who require disability-related accommodations must contact the [Georgia Tech Office of Disability Services](https://disabilityservices.gatech.edu/) as early as possible. Students must complete the official registration process, provide any documentation requested by the Office of Disability Services, and obtain an approved Faculty Notification Letter.

After accommodations have been approved, students are responsible for requesting that their Faculty Notification Letter be sent to the instructor through the official Georgia Tech system.

The ML instructional team cannot approve disability-related accommodations based only on an email, an Ed Discussion post, an informal request, or medical documentation sent directly by a student. **Students should not send medical records, diagnostic reports, or other private health information to the instructor or teaching assistants.**

Approved accommodations will be implemented after the instructional team receives the official Faculty Notification Letter and has sufficient time to coordinate the accommodation. Students are therefore strongly encouraged to begin this process as early as possible.

## Academic Integrity

- All students are expected to know and comply with the [Georgia Tech Academic Honor Code](https://policylibrary.gatech.edu/student-life/academic-honor-code) and the [Georgia Tech Student Code of Conduct](https://policylibrary.gatech.edu/student-life/student-code-conduct).
- Ethical conduct, honesty, and proper acknowledgment of the work of others are essential requirements of this course.
- Unless collaboration is explicitly permitted for a particular course component, all submitted work must represent the student's own understanding and effort.

> 1. **Quizzes and examinations are strictly individual assessments.** Students may not communicate with another person, receive assistance from another person, use generative-AI systems, or access any resource that has not been explicitly authorized by the instructional team.
>
> 2. Students may discuss the optional problem sets at the **high-level or whiteboard level**. For example, students may discuss the meaning of cross-validation or the general purpose of a data structure. Students may not exchange complete solutions, complete derivations, complete code, screenshots of answers, or instructions that effectively reveal the full solution.
>
> 3. The semester project is a team assignment. Collaboration is permitted among the officially registered members of the team. Each team member must nevertheless understand the entire project and may be asked questions about **any part of the project**, not only the portion they personally completed.
>
> 4. **Plagiarism is a serious offense.** Students may not copy, lightly rewrite, paraphrase, or submit text, code, figures, experimental results, ideas, or other materials created by another person or source as though they created them. Any outside material used in a permitted manner must be clearly and accurately acknowledged.
>
> 5. **Every citation must be genuine and verifiable.** A citation must refer to a real source that the student or team has actually reviewed, and the source must support the claim for which it is cited. Invented references, nonexistent articles, fabricated authors or publication details, inaccessible fake links, and citations that materially misrepresent a source are prohibited. Fabricated citations may constitute plagiarism, deliberate falsification, or false claims of performance.
>
> 6. When generative AI is permitted for the semester project, students remain fully responsible for every statement, citation, calculation, result, and piece of code in the submission. Generative AI must be used and disclosed only as permitted by the project policy. Claiming that an AI system generated an incorrect or fabricated reference is not a defense.
>
> 7. Students may not place problem-set solutions, quiz or examination questions, autograder information, course-created code, lecture materials, or other protected course content in a public repository or distribute them through websites, messaging platforms, tutoring services, file-sharing systems, or other public or private channels. Project code may be shared only when and in the manner specifically permitted by the project instructions.
>
> 8. **Attempting to bypass Honorlock or any other examination-security requirement is a serious academic-integrity violation.** Prohibited actions include, but are not limited to, using a virtual machine, remote desktop, screen-sharing application, second operating-system session, external monitor, secondary computer, phone, tablet, unauthorized browser extension, unauthorized website, or any other technology or method intended to evade monitoring or obtain unauthorized assistance.
>
> 9. **Suspected attempts to bypass Honorlock, use unauthorized resources, share assessment content, fabricate citations, falsify project results, or otherwise violate the course or Institute academic-integrity rules will be referred to the [Georgia Tech Office of Student Integrity (OSI)](https://osi.gatech.edu/).**
>
> 10. Academic-misconduct consequences are determined through Georgia Tech's official procedures. Depending on the circumstances and the student's conduct history, consequences may include a zero on the affected assessment, failure in the course, disciplinary probation, suspension, or expulsion from Georgia Tech. Students should review the [Academic Misconduct Sanctioning Guidelines](https://osi.gatech.edu/process/academic-misconduct-sanctioning-guidelines).



## Respectful Online Conduct

Students should communicate professionally and respectfully. Harassment, discrimination, threats, retaliation, or disclosure of another student's private information is not acceptable. Disagreement about technical ideas is welcome when expressed constructively and supported by reasoning or evidence.

## Policy and Schedule Changes

The teaching team may revise course details when necessary. Official changes will be announced on Ed Discussion and reflected in Canvas. Students are responsible for the latest official announcement.
