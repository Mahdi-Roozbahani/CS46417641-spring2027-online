Project Proposal and Team Charter - 5% #

The primary goal of the proposal is to define a meaningful machine-learning, artificial-intelligence, or deep-learning problem and establish a feasible plan for completing the project during the semester.

The proposal should identify the problem, relevant prior work, data, proposed methods, evaluation strategy, major risks, and responsibilities of each team member.

Research projects naturally evolve. The final project does not need to remain identical to the original proposal. However, any significant change must be clearly explained and justified in the midterm and final reports.

Proposal Sections and Checklist #

1. Introduction, Background, and Verified Prior Work #

Introduce the project area and provide enough background for a reader to understand the context of the proposed work.

Summarize the most relevant prior work and explain how it informs the project. Do not merely list papers. Explain what the team learned from them and how they influence the proposed methods, baselines, data, or evaluation plan.

✅ Clear Project Context

✅ Relevant Literature Review

✅ At Least 3 Credible References

✅ In-Text Citation for Every Listed Reference

✅ Citation Verification Table

Every cited source must exist, must have been opened and reviewed by at least one team member, and must support the statement for which it is cited.

2. Problem Definition and Motivation #

Clearly identify the problem that the team intends to address.

Explain:

  • What is the technical or applied problem?
  • Why is the problem meaningful?
  • Who or what could benefit from a successful result?
  • What decision, prediction, recommendation, generation, analysis, or action will the system support?
  • What would count as a useful or successful outcome?

✅ Clear Problem Statement

✅ Meaningful Motivation

✅ Intended Users, Stakeholders, or Application

✅ Definition of a Useful Outcome

A project is not automatically meaningful merely because it uses an AI API or a large pretrained model. The proposal must identify a substantive technical question and a clear evaluation plan.

3. Data and Feasibility #

Identify the dataset, simulator, benchmark, collection process, or evaluation environment that the team plans to use.

Describe:

  • The source of the data.
  • The approximate number of examples.
  • Important variables, labels, modalities, or time ranges.
  • Data-cleaning or preprocessing needs.
  • Known limitations or biases.
  • Access, privacy, licensing, and ethical considerations.
  • The main risks that could prevent the project from being completed.

Provide a stable dataset or documentation link when one is available.

✅ Data Source Identified

✅ Dataset Link or Access Procedure

✅ Dataset Characteristics Described

✅ Privacy and Licensing Considered

✅ Major Feasibility Risks Identified

Teams must verify that they are legally and ethically permitted to access, process, store, and publish the proposed data.

4. Proposed Methods and Baselines #

Describe the initial technical approach.

The proposal should include:

  • At least one meaningful baseline.
  • Planned preprocessing or feature-engineering methods.
  • One or more proposed ML, AI, or deep-learning methods.
  • A justification for why the proposed methods are appropriate.
  • The primary technical comparison, hypothesis, or research question.

✅ Meaningful Baseline Identified

✅ Initial Preprocessing Plan

✅ Proposed Models or Technical Approach

✅ Justification for Method Selection

✅ Clear Technical Question or Comparison

Do not list many models merely to make the proposal appear more advanced. Explain what the team expects to learn from each planned comparison.

Using established libraries and existing implementations is permitted and normally recommended. Teams must cite and comply with the licenses of external software, pretrained models, APIs, and code.

5. Evaluation Plan and Expected Results #

Define how the project will be evaluated.

Explain:

  • The primary quantitative and, when appropriate, qualitative metrics.
  • Why the selected metrics match the project objective.
  • The proposed train, validation, and test procedure or other evaluation design.
  • The baselines or operating conditions that will be compared.
  • Potential sources of leakage, bias, confounding, or invalid comparison.
  • What evidence would support or reject the team’s main hypothesis.
  • The team’s initial expectations.

✅ Appropriate Evaluation Metrics

✅ Validation and Testing Procedure

✅ Leakage and Bias Risks Considered

✅ Comparison Plan

✅ Expected Results or Hypotheses

Accuracy alone may not be sufficient. The selected evaluation must reflect the actual goals, risks, and operating conditions of the project.

6. Team Charter and Work Plan #

The proposal must include a team charter that establishes how the team will work together.

Include:

  • Names of all officially registered team members.
  • A recurring meeting schedule.
  • The primary communication channel.
  • Expected response times.
  • Expected weekly contribution.
  • Initial roles and responsibilities.
  • Repository and document-access procedures.
  • A process for making team decisions.
  • A process for handling missed commitments.
  • A process for handling disagreements.
  • A semester milestone plan or Gantt chart.

✅ All Team Members Listed

✅ Weekly Meeting Plan

✅ Communication Expectations

✅ Initial Responsibilities

✅ Repository and Document Access

✅ Decision and Conflict Procedures

✅ Gantt Chart or Milestone Plan

Roles may change during the semester. However, every team member remains responsible for understanding the complete project.

7. Generative-AI Plan #

State whether and how the team expects to use generative AI.

Possible uses include brainstorming, coding, debugging, literature discovery, writing support, analysis, visualization, or interface development.

The plan should identify:

  • Anticipated uses of generative AI.
  • How AI-generated code, citations, claims, and results will be verified.
  • Privacy, licensing, security, or data risks.
  • How the team will document AI use.
  • Which responsibilities will remain directly with the team members.

✅ Anticipated AI Uses Identified

✅ Verification Procedure

✅ Privacy and Licensing Risks Considered

✅ AI Disclosure Plan

Generative AI may assist the team, but the team remains responsible for every submitted statement, citation, result, calculation, and piece of code.

Proposal Submission Requirements #

1. Project Website and Proposal Report #

The proposal must be published on the team’s project website or proposal page and must be accessible to the instructional team.

The proposal text should contain approximately 600 to 1,000 words, excluding references, tables, the Citation Verification Table, contribution table, team charter, and Gantt chart.

The page must contain all seven proposal sections listed above.

✅ Proposal Website Accessible to Course Staff

✅ Approximately 600 to 1,000 Words

✅ All Required Proposal Sections

✅ Working Links and Legible Figures

2. Team Charter and Planning Materials #

Include:

  • The complete team charter.
  • A semester Gantt chart or milestone plan.
  • A proposal contribution table identifying each member’s contribution to preparing the proposal.

A contribution table may use the following format:

Team member Proposal contributions
Member 1 Specific contributions
Member 2 Specific contributions
Member 3 Specific contributions
Member 4 Specific contributions
Member 5 Specific contributions

✅ Team Charter

✅ Gantt Chart or Milestone Plan

✅ Proposal Contribution Table

3. Citation Verification Table #

For every cited source, include:

  • Complete citation.
  • Stable URL or DOI when available.
  • Name of the team member who verified it.
  • Date verified.
  • The project claim, method, dataset, or decision supported by the source.

✅ Every Citation Verified

✅ Every Reference Connected to an In-Text Citation

✅ No Fabricated or Misleading References

Fake or fabricated references are prohibited. AI-generated citations must be checked against the original source before submission.

4. Project Repository #

Create a private, course-approved repository and provide access to:

  • Every team member.
  • The assigned project TA.
  • Other instructional-team members when requested.

At the proposal stage, the repository may contain only preliminary materials. It must nevertheless be accessible before the submission deadline.

✅ Private Repository Created

✅ All Team Members Have Access

✅ Assigned TA Has Access

5. Canvas Submission #

Submit all required links and materials through the designated Canvas assignment.

The Canvas submission should include:

  • Project website or proposal-page link.
  • Repository link.
  • Citation Verification Table.
  • Team charter and Gantt chart.
  • Any other item identified in the Canvas instructions.

A link that is private, broken, or inaccessible to the instructional team will be treated as missing until access is corrected.
