Team Final Report, Website, and TA Interview #

The team delivers a paper-style report on the same team website, supporting code and reproduction instructions, and a TA interview. The interview is in the two-week final-review window; the completed website is due on the last instructional day shown in the course schedule. Keep the same ML Class Hub team link. The individual project’s final website and interview are separate; see Individual Project.

Report structure #

  1. Question, motivation, and contribution: state what you studied and what is new or useful relative to verified prior work. Do not claim publication or novelty without evidence.
  2. Data and responsible use: source, license, preparation, splits, privacy considerations, and limitations of the dataset.
  3. Methods and baselines: enough detail to understand and reproduce the implementation and why each comparison is fair.
  4. Experiments and results: metrics, tables or figures, comparisons, ablations or robustness checks where relevant, and uncertainty when the data support it.
  5. Analysis: explain successes, failures, alternative explanations, and what the results do and do not establish.
  6. Limitations and future work: describe remaining weaknesses and the next experiment that would most improve the evidence.
  7. References, contributions, and AI disclosure: sources actually reviewed, each member’s work, and the tools, purpose, and scope of any generative-AI assistance.

Provide accessible code, environment and dependency information, data-access instructions or a lawful substitute, and steps to reproduce the key results. Do not publish private, restricted, or licensed data merely to make reproduction easier. A working interactive demo can strengthen the project when it is relevant, but a polished interface does not substitute for valid ML evidence.

Final TA interview #

Bring a working core algorithm, baseline, and preliminary results to the interview. Show the website and code, explain the mathematics and experimental choices, and answer questions about results and failure cases. Every team member must understand the whole project. Use the TA’s feedback to finish the website by the instructional-day deadline. A later research paper, open-source release, or portfolio entry can grow from this work, but is not required for course completion.
