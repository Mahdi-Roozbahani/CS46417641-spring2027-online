Final Project Submission and Presentation - 35% #

The final project stage consists of two required components:

Component Course weight
Final website, report, code, and reproducibility package 25%
Final presentation and individual project-wide Q&A 10%
Final-stage total 35%

The final submission should present a complete, carefully evaluated project and clearly explain what the team attempted, what was implemented, what evidence was obtained, what failed, and what was learned.

The final report should update and extend the midterm report. It should not simply append new results to outdated proposal or midterm text.

Final Report Sections and Checklist #

1. Introduction, Background, and Problem Definition #

Present the final version of:

  • Project motivation.
  • Problem definition.
  • Intended users, stakeholders, or application.
  • Relevant prior work.
  • Technical question or hypothesis.
  • Definition of a useful outcome.

✅ Clear Final Problem Statement

✅ Meaningful Motivation

✅ Verified Prior Work

✅ Project Objective and Success Criteria

The introduction should reflect the project that was actually completed.

2. Data and Responsible Data Use #

Describe the final data used in the project.

Include:

  • Data source and access procedure.
  • Size, variables, labels, and modalities.
  • Collection, filtering, cleaning, labeling, and preprocessing.
  • Train, validation, and test construction.
  • Known limitations and sources of bias.
  • Privacy, licensing, and ethical considerations.
  • Any differences from the data originally proposed.

✅ Final Dataset Description

✅ Data Processing Documented

✅ Evaluation Splits Explained

✅ Limitations and Biases Identified

✅ Privacy and Licensing Requirements Satisfied

3. Methods and Technical Approach #

Describe all important methods used in the final project.

Include:

  • Meaningful baselines.
  • Preprocessing and feature engineering.
  • Models, architectures, algorithms, or system components.
  • Hyperparameters and training procedures.
  • Technical modifications or integrations.
  • Reasons for selecting each approach.
  • Important implementation details.

✅ Meaningful Baselines

✅ Final Methods Clearly Explained

✅ Important Hyperparameters Documented

✅ Method Choices Justified

✅ Technical Implementation Connected to the Project Question

The final analysis should normally compare at least three meaningful approaches, baselines, model variants, ablations, or experimental conditions.

A project centered on one advanced system may instead use strong baselines, component ablations, model variants, operating conditions, or other rigorous comparisons. Merely changing small hyperparameters does not automatically create a meaningful comparison.

4. Experimental Design #

Explain how the team designed the experiments.

Include:

  • Primary metrics and why they are appropriate.
  • Comparison methodology.
  • Validation and testing procedures.
  • Measures taken to avoid leakage or invalid comparisons.
  • Repeated trials, statistical analysis, or uncertainty analysis when appropriate.
  • Computational or deployment conditions that affect interpretation.
  • Evaluation of an interactive system or decision-support tool when applicable.

✅ Appropriate Metrics

✅ Valid Comparison Procedure

✅ Leakage Prevention

✅ Reproducible Experimental Design

✅ Uncertainty or Variability Considered When Appropriate

5. Results and Discussion #

Present and interpret the final results.

Include:

  • Quantitative results.
  • Useful figures, tables, or interactive visualizations.
  • Comparisons among methods or experimental conditions.
  • Strengths and weaknesses of each approach.
  • Error and failure-case analysis.
  • Unexpected results.
  • Evidence supporting or rejecting the main hypothesis.
  • Practical meaning of the results.

✅ Quantitative Results

✅ Effective Visualizations

✅ Meaningful Comparisons

✅ Error and Failure Analysis

✅ Results Interpreted Rather Than Merely Reported

✅ Conclusions Supported by Evidence

High performance alone does not guarantee a strong project. The report must demonstrate careful evaluation and understanding.

6. Limitations and Responsible AI #

Discuss limitations honestly.

Consider:

  • Dataset limitations.
  • Generalization and distribution shift.
  • Fairness or bias.
  • Privacy.
  • Security and misuse.
  • Reliability and uncertainty.
  • Explainability.
  • Human oversight.
  • Computational or environmental cost.
  • Deployment risks.
  • Conditions under which the system should not be used.

✅ Technical Limitations

✅ Data Limitations

✅ Responsible-AI Considerations

✅ Appropriate Use and Misuse Boundaries

A short generic ethics paragraph is not sufficient. The discussion should be connected to the team’s actual system and evidence.

7. Conclusion and Future Work #

Summarize:

  • What the team accomplished.
  • The strongest evidence.
  • The main technical lesson.
  • The largest limitation.
  • The most valuable next step.

✅ Clear Conclusion

✅ Claims Match the Evidence

✅ Realistic Future Work

8. References and Citation Verification #

Every cited source must exist, must have been reviewed by at least one team member, and must support the statement for which it is cited.

The final Citation Verification Table must include all sources used in the website, report, code documentation, presentation, and interactive artifact.

✅ Complete Reference List

✅ In-Text Citation for Every Reference

✅ Complete Citation Verification Table

✅ Accurate Authors, Titles, Venues, URLs, and DOIs

✅ No Fabricated or Misleading Citations

The teaching team may select any cited source and ask any team member to explain its main contribution and relationship to the project.

Fabricated citations, fake quotations, invented publication details, or citations that materially misrepresent a source are prohibited and may be referred to the Georgia Tech Office of Student Integrity.

9. Team Contributions and Generative-AI Disclosure #

Include a final contribution table describing each team member’s specific work throughout the project.

Also include a complete generative-AI disclosure describing:

  • Which tools were used.
  • How they were used.
  • Which outputs were accepted, modified, or rejected.
  • How generated code, citations, claims, and results were verified.
  • Any relevant privacy, licensing, or security concerns.

✅ Final Contribution Table

✅ Complete GenAI Disclosure

✅ AI-Generated Citations and Claims Verified

✅ Team Accepts Responsibility for All Submitted Work

“The AI produced it” is not a defense for an error, fake citation, unsupported claim, invalid result, or academic-integrity violation.

Final Submission Requirements #

1. Final Project Website and Report - Part of the 25% #

The final project must be presented through a clear and professional project website.

The website should contain:

  • Introduction and background.
  • Problem definition.
  • Data.
  • Methods.
  • Experimental design.
  • Results and visualizations.
  • Failure analysis.
  • Limitations and responsible-AI discussion.
  • Conclusions.
  • References.
  • Team contributions.
  • Generative-AI disclosure.

✅ Final Website Accessible

✅ All Required Sections Included

✅ Professional and Legible Presentation

✅ Figures and Tables Explained

✅ Links and Interactive Elements Working

There is no fixed word limit. However, the report should be concise enough to remain readable and detailed enough to support the technical claims.

2. Interactive Website, Visualization, or ML/AI Platform #

Teams are strongly encouraged to go beyond a static report website by creating an interactive visualization, web application, decision-support tool, recommendation platform, model demonstration, or other usable system.

A strong interactive artifact may allow a user to:

  • Enter or upload new data.
  • Run the team’s model.
  • Explore predictions or generated outputs.
  • Compare scenarios.
  • Visualize uncertainty or explanations.
  • Receive a recommendation, warning, ranking, intervention, or next-best action.
  • Use the system as part of a meaningful workflow.

✅ Interactive Demonstration Encouraged

✅ Working Software or Platform Encouraged

✅ User Actions Connected to the Team's Actual Model

An advanced interactive platform is one of the strongest paths to earning optional project bonus credit. A static page, nonfunctional mockup, or basic wrapper around an existing API is not sufficient for advanced bonus credit.

3. Code and Reproducibility Package - Part of the 25% #

The final repository must include:

  • Complete source code.
  • Data-preparation code or approved access instructions.
  • Training and evaluation code.
  • Configuration files.
  • Dependency or environment information.
  • Model or checkpoint access instructions when appropriate.
  • A complete README.md.
  • Instructions for reproducing the main reported results.
  • Instructions for running the interactive system when applicable.
  • Appropriate licenses and acknowledgments.

✅ Complete Code

✅ Complete README

✅ Environment and Dependency Information

✅ Reproduction Instructions

✅ Main Results Reproducible

✅ Course Staff Have Repository Access

Do not publicly distribute protected data, private information, licensed materials, or content that the team does not have permission to share.

4. Final Contribution and Verification Materials #

Include:

  • Final contribution table.
  • Final Citation Verification Table.
  • Final generative-AI disclosure.
  • Any required model, dataset, software, or licensing acknowledgments.
  • Advanced Project Bonus section when bonus credit is requested.

Final Presentation and Q&A - 10% #

The final presentation is the second of exactly two required TA interactions.

Each team will complete a live presentation with its assigned project TA.

The normal format is:

  • Project presentation: approximately 9 minutes.
  • Individual project-wide Q&A: approximately 3 minutes or as directed by the TA.

All team members must attend and participate.

Presentation Content #

The presentation should concisely communicate:

  • The problem and motivation.
  • The data.
  • The methods and baselines.
  • The experimental design.
  • The main results.
  • Meaningful comparisons.
  • Failure cases and limitations.
  • The final system, website, or interactive demonstration.
  • The team’s main conclusions.

✅ Clear Introduction

✅ Appropriate Technical Detail

✅ Effective Results and Visualizations

✅ Meaningful Model or Method Comparisons

✅ Limitations Discussed

✅ Presentation Completed Within the Time Limit

Interactive demonstrations are strongly encouraged when they are stable and directly support the project’s technical contribution.

Individual Project-Wide Q&A #

The TA will ask questions directly to individual team members.

A question may cover any part of the project, including:

  • Problem definition.
  • Data and preprocessing.
  • Baselines and methods.
  • Code and system design.
  • Metrics and experimental design.
  • Results and failure cases.
  • Limitations.
  • Generative-AI use.
  • Any cited paper, dataset, API, software package, or other source.

Every team member must understand the complete project. A student may not answer, “That was another member’s part,” as a substitute for understanding.

The individual Q&A score is assigned separately to each student and may differ among members of the same team.

A student who cannot reasonably explain the project or answer the TA’s questions may receive a lower individual project grade than the other team members.

Forty-Eight-Hour Follow-Up Video #

When a student’s live answer is incomplete or incorrect, the TA may, at the TA’s discretion, allow one follow-up video.

The TA will identify the specific questions or topics that must be addressed.

The video must:

  • Be submitted within 48 hours of the scheduled presentation.
  • Show the student on camera.
  • Answer the assigned questions.
  • Use the student’s own words.
  • Demonstrate understanding rather than simply reading prepared text.
  • Be submitted privately using the method specified by the TA.

A follow-up video may recover up to 50% of the individual Q&A points originally lost. It cannot increase the student’s individual Q&A score above full credit.

The follow-up opportunity is not automatic and is not a substitute for preparing for the live presentation.

Absence from the Final Presentation #

A student who misses the scheduled presentation remains responsible for demonstrating complete project understanding.

The student must notify the assigned TA through a private Ed Discussion post and submit an individual replacement video within 48 hours of the scheduled presentation, unless a different deadline is officially approved.

The replacement video must provide a thorough explanation of:

  • The complete project.
  • Problem and motivation.
  • Data and preprocessing.
  • Methods and baselines.
  • Evaluation and metrics.
  • Results and failure cases.
  • Limitations.
  • The student’s contributions.
  • Generative-AI use.
  • Any additional questions assigned by the TA.

An officially approved absence may be eligible for full individual presentation and Q&A credit through the replacement process.

Submitting a replacement video does not excuse an unapproved absence. A student who misses the live presentation without an officially approved exception may lose attendance or participation credit even when a video is submitted.

Final Presentation Rubric - 10 Course Points #

Criterion Scope Points
Technical content, organization, timing, visualizations, and demonstration Team 6.0
Ability to answer project-wide questions and demonstrate genuine understanding Individual 4.0
Total 10.0

The 6-point team score is normally shared by all team members.

The 4-point individual Q&A score is assigned separately to each student.
