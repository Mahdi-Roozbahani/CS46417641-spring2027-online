Quizzes and Exams #

Assessment Summary #

Assessment Number Weight Permitted Materials
Quiz 0 1 0% Honorlock and course-readiness practice
Graded quizzes 14 20% total Closed book and closed notes
Midterm exam 1 12.5% Approved online reference sheet only
Final exam 1 12.5% Approved online reference sheet only

All graded quizzes and examinations are completed in Canvas and proctored with Honorlock unless an officially approved accommodation specifies a different process.

Exact availability dates, durations, and deadlines will be posted in Canvas. Course-wide announcements and changes will be communicated through Ed Discussion. Students are responsible for enabling Ed Discussion notifications and checking course announcements regularly.

Anywhere on Earth Deadlines #

Unless an official Ed Discussion announcement states otherwise, assessment deadlines identified as AoE are due at 23:59 Anywhere on Earth, abbreviated AoE, on the listed calendar date.

AoE is equivalent to UTC−12. It allows students throughout the world to use the full listed calendar date.

For example, a deadline of Monday at 23:59 AoE corresponds to:

  • Tuesday at 7:59 a.m. Eastern Daylight Time, EDT, when Atlanta is observing daylight-saving time.
  • Tuesday at 6:59 a.m. Eastern Standard Time, EST, when Atlanta is observing standard time.

Therefore, a Monday AoE deadline will normally appear as Tuesday morning for students in Atlanta and elsewhere in the Eastern Time Zone.

Canvas may display the equivalent deadline in the time zone configured in the student’s Canvas account. Students are responsible for confirming that their Canvas time-zone settings are correct and for checking the exact deadline shown in Canvas well before an assessment closes.

Time-zone mistakes, daylight-saving changes, travel, incorrectly configured Canvas settings, or incorrect personal-calendar conversions do not create an automatic extension.

Quiz Sources and Expectations #

Quiz questions are based on the assigned lecture videos, lecture slides, written lecture notes, and the instructor’s annotations and review notes.

Students should study all material presented in the assigned lectures, including:

  • Content displayed on the lecture slides.
  • Written annotations and notes added to the slides.
  • Explanations provided verbally in the lecture videos.
  • Review material presented at the beginning of later lectures.
  • Examples used to explain important concepts.

We will not intentionally assess topics that are outside the assigned lecture videos, slides, and written notes.

Quizzes are intended primarily to test conceptual understanding. They may include short calculations, interpretation of equations, model behavior, comparison of methods, or reasoning about examples. They are not designed around long or computationally heavy calculations.

Practice quizzes may be provided for many graded quizzes. Practice quizzes are useful for checking understanding and becoming familiar with the question style, but they do not define or limit the graded quiz question bank.

Students should carefully review their performance on earlier quizzes after feedback becomes available. Understanding why an earlier answer was incorrect is one of the most important ways to prepare for later assessments.

The four optional problem sets provide additional practice. However, the problem sets are supplementary resources. They do not replace the lecture videos, slides, or written notes, and they do not define the complete set of topics or questions that may appear on a quiz or examination.

Quiz Availability #

Each graded quiz will normally become available on Thursday afternoon, Eastern Time, which is Atlanta time. The exact opening time will be listed in Canvas.

Each graded quiz will normally close on Monday at 23:59 AoE.

This means that the quiz will normally remain available until Tuesday morning in Atlanta:

  • Tuesday at 7:59 a.m. EDT during daylight-saving time.
  • Tuesday at 6:59 a.m. EST during standard time.

Students may begin the quiz at any time during the availability window. However, each quiz permits only one attempt.

Once a student begins a quiz:

  • The attempt has officially started.
  • The quiz timer runs continuously.
  • The timer cannot be paused.
  • Leaving Canvas, closing the browser, losing focus, or navigating away does not stop the timer.
  • The student may not restart the quiz or receive a new attempt merely because the quiz was opened unintentionally.

Students should not click the button to begin a quiz until they are fully prepared to complete it.

Students must begin early enough to receive the full allotted time before the quiz closes. If a student begins near the closing deadline, Canvas may automatically submit the attempt when the availability window closes, even if time would otherwise remain on the student’s timer.

Quiz Rules #

Graded quizzes are closed book and closed notes.

The following are not permitted:

  • Reference sheets.
  • Printed or handwritten notes.
  • Lecture videos.
  • Lecture slides or lecture-note tabs.
  • Problem sets.
  • External websites or search engines.
  • Generative-AI systems.
  • Coding assistants.
  • Translation, calculation, or communication tools unless explicitly authorized.
  • Help from another person.
  • Phones, tablets, secondary computers, or other secondary devices.
  • External monitors.
  • Virtual machines, remote desktops, or screen-sharing software.
  • Any resource not explicitly authorized by the instructional team.

Students may use only the computer and Honorlock configuration approved for the quiz.

Students must not discuss, reproduce, photograph, record, post, transmit, or share quiz questions or related assessment content while the quiz window remains open. This restriction applies to public and private communication, including messages to classmates or students who have not yet taken the quiz.

Exact quiz durations and question counts are listed in Canvas.

Quiz 0 and Honorlock Preparation #

Quiz 0 is an ungraded practice assessment intended to verify that the student’s computer, browser, camera, microphone, network connection, and Honorlock configuration work properly before graded assessments begin.

Students must complete Quiz 0 using the same type of computer, browser, internet connection, and testing environment they plan to use for graded quizzes and examinations.

Students should complete Quiz 0 again whenever they make a significant change to their:

  • Computer.
  • Operating system.
  • Browser.
  • Browser extensions.
  • Camera or microphone.
  • Network connection.
  • Physical testing location.

If Canvas requests an access code when a student attempts to begin an Honorlock assessment, Honorlock has probably not been launched or configured correctly. Students should not request an access code from the instructional team. They should confirm that the Honorlock browser extension is properly installed and launch the assessment through the Honorlock process.

External monitors must be disconnected before beginning Quiz 0 or any graded assessment.

Students experiencing an Honorlock problem should use Honorlock live support before beginning the assessment whenever possible.

Examination Availability and the One-Attempt Rule #

The midterm and final examinations will each be available for a seven-day examination window. The exact opening and closing dates and times will be posted in Canvas and announced on Ed Discussion.

Students may begin an examination at any time during its seven-day availability window. Students are not required to begin at the same time as other students.

However, each examination permits only one attempt.

Once a student begins an examination:

  • The examination timer starts immediately.
  • The timer runs continuously.
  • The timer cannot be paused.
  • The examination must be completed in one continuous attempt.
  • Navigating away, closing the browser, or leaving the testing area does not stop the timer.
  • A student may not preview the examination and return later.
  • A student may not restart the examination or request another attempt because the examination was opened accidentally.

Students should begin only when they are fully prepared, have sufficient uninterrupted time, and have confirmed that their equipment and Honorlock setup are working properly.

Students must begin the examination early enough to receive the full allotted time before the examination window closes. If a student begins too close to the closing deadline, Canvas may automatically submit the attempt when the examination window closes, even if time remains on the student’s individual timer.

The seven-day availability window provides flexibility for students in different time zones and with different schedules. It does not permit multiple attempts, pausing, restarting, or extending an attempt across multiple sessions.

Students must not discuss, reproduce, photograph, record, post, transmit, or share examination questions or related content until the entire examination window has closed for all students.

Examination Sources #

The primary sources for examination questions are:

  • Assigned lecture videos.
  • Lecture slides.
  • Written lecture notes and annotations.
  • Explanations and examples presented during the lectures.
  • Review material presented in later lectures.

The optional problem sets provide additional practice and can help students better understand important concepts. However, the examinations are not restricted to questions appearing in the problem sets.

Problem Sets 1 and 2 provide additional preparation for the midterm examination.

Problem Sets 3 and 4 provide additional preparation for the final examination.

Official problem-set solutions will not be released. Students are responsible for using the Gradescope autograder feedback, reviewing the lecture material, and asking conceptual questions through Ed Discussion to improve their understanding.

Midterm Coverage #

The midterm examination covers all course material from the beginning of the semester through and including dimensionality reduction.

Dimensionality reduction is included on the midterm examination. Topics introduced after dimensionality reduction are not included unless the instructional team announces a course-wide change on Ed Discussion.

The primary study sources are the first-half lecture videos, lecture slides, and written notes. Problem Sets 1 and 2 are supplementary practice resources.

The midterm may ask students to:

  • Explain or interpret a concept.
  • Compare two methods.
  • Analyze model behavior.
  • Interpret an equation or result.
  • Perform a limited calculation.
  • Apply a concept to a new example.
  • Identify why a method succeeds or fails under particular conditions.

The midterm is not restricted to questions or examples that previously appeared in the problem sets or practice quizzes.

Final Examination Coverage #

The final examination is based 100% on material taught after the midterm.

This means that the topics being directly assessed will come from the post-midterm portion of the course.

However, post-midterm topics rely on foundational knowledge introduced earlier in the course. Students are expected to retain and apply the linear algebra, probability, statistics, optimization principles, and foundational equations needed to understand and solve questions involving post-midterm material.

Earlier concepts may therefore be required as tools for answering a post-midterm question. The final will not include separate questions whose primary purpose is to reassess a standalone first-half topic.

Problem Sets 3 and 4 provide supplementary practice for the final. The post-midterm lecture videos, lecture slides, and written notes remain the primary sources.

Approved Online Reference Sheet for the Midterm and Final #

Each student may prepare one PDF containing at most two pages for the midterm and one separate PDF containing at most two pages for the final.

Two PDF pages are intended to be equivalent to one physical letter-size sheet used on both sides.

The reference sheet is permitted only for the midterm and final examinations. It is not permitted for graded quizzes.

Reference-Sheet File Requirements #

The reference sheet must satisfy all of the following requirements:

  • It must be a single PDF file.
  • It may contain no more than two pages.
  • Pages must be letter size or A4.
  • The content must be prepared by the student.
  • Typed content is permitted.
  • Handwritten content scanned into the PDF is permitted.
  • A combination of typed and handwritten content is permitted.
  • The PDF must not contain hyperlinks.
  • The PDF must not contain embedded files.
  • The PDF must not contain scripts, audio, video, animations, or interactive content.
  • The PDF must be readable at a normal on-screen magnification.

Reference-Sheet Submission #

Students must upload the reference-sheet PDF to the designated Canvas assignment before the posted submission deadline.

The reference-sheet deadline will occur before the corresponding examination window opens.

The uploaded file becomes the student’s only approved reference sheet for that examination. Students may not replace, edit, or resubmit the file after the deadline unless the instructional team provides a course-wide exception.

A student who does not submit a reference sheet by the deadline must take the examination without one.

Reference-Sheet Use During the Examination #

During the examination, students may access the approved reference sheet only through the Canvas and Honorlock workflow specified by the instructional team.

Students may not use:

  • A printed copy.
  • A locally saved copy.
  • A cloud-drive copy.
  • A copy stored in email.
  • A phone or tablet copy.
  • A copy displayed on a second computer.
  • A copy displayed on an external monitor.
  • Another student’s reference sheet.
  • Any version different from the file submitted before the deadline.

The approved reference sheet must remain on the monitored computer screen. During the examination, the only permitted electronic content is the Canvas examination and the approved Canvas reference sheet.

The instructional team will provide a practice workflow before the midterm. Students are responsible for completing the practice workflow and confirming that they can access the approved reference sheet properly before beginning the actual examination.

Office of Disability Services Accommodations #

Students requiring disability-related accommodations must first complete the official process through the Georgia Tech Office of Disability Services and request that an official Faculty Notification Letter be sent to the instructor.

The instructional team cannot approve disability-related accommodations based only on an email, Ed Discussion post, informal request, or medical documentation sent directly by a student.

Students must not send medical records, diagnostic reports, or other private health information to the instructor, teaching assistants, or Ed Discussion.

After the Faculty Notification Letter has been sent, the student should notify the instructional team through a private Ed Discussion post sufficiently early to allow the approved accommodation to be implemented.

Students should complete this process at least 10 working days before the relevant examination window opens. Given the size of the course, the instructional team may be unable to implement last-minute accommodation requests when the official process was not completed sufficiently early.

Make-up Examinations #

Examinations must be completed during their scheduled seven-day availability windows.

Because each examination is available for a full week, make-up examinations will be considered only under the limited, officially documented circumstances described below.

Accepted Exceptions and Required Documentation #

The only acceptable bases for a make-up examination are:

  • Institute-approved absences: The absence must be supported by an official Institute Approved Absence notification.
  • Medical emergencies or serious personal emergencies: The circumstances must be supported by an official letter or notification from the Georgia Tech Office of the Dean of Students.

The official documentation must establish that the approved circumstance prevented the student from reasonably completing the examination during its scheduled availability window.

Students must first complete the appropriate Georgia Tech process before requesting consideration from the ML instructional team.

After contacting the appropriate Georgia Tech office, the student must notify the instructional team through a private Ed Discussion post. The private post should:

  1. Confirm that the appropriate official Georgia Tech process has been initiated.
  2. Identify the affected examination.
  3. Identify the relevant examination availability window.
  4. State that an official notification or supporting letter has been requested.

Students must not upload medical records, hospital documents, diagnostic information, or other sensitive personal information to Ed Discussion or send such documents directly to the instructor or teaching assistants.

A private Ed Discussion post does not, by itself, approve a make-up examination. The request can be reviewed only after the instructional team receives the appropriate official Georgia Tech notification.

The supporting letter or official notification must be received within one week of the end of the affected examination window. If the examination window closed more than one week earlier, the instructional team will normally be unable to provide a retroactive make-up examination.

Approved Make-up Examination #

If a make-up examination is approved, the student must complete it during a single fixed make-up examination window determined by the instructional team.

The date and time of the make-up window will be communicated only to approved students.

Students may not select an individual make-up date, request a different sitting, or negotiate a personal examination window.

The make-up examination may contain different questions and may use a different format from the original examination while assessing the same course learning objectives.

Reasons That Are Not Accepted #

Any reason not supported by an official Institute Approved Absence notification or an official Dean of Students letter or notification is not an acceptable basis for a make-up examination.

Nonaccepted reasons include, but are not limited to:

  • Job or internship interviews.
  • Ordinary work conflicts.
  • Personal travel or vacations.
  • Conferences not covered by an Institute Approved Absence.
  • Preparation for another examination.
  • Competing academic or professional deadlines.
  • Time-zone mistakes.
  • Incorrect Canvas time-zone settings.
  • Forgotten examination dates.
  • Personal scheduling conflicts.
  • Beginning the examination too close to the closing deadline.
  • Accidentally opening the examination.
  • Routine technical problems that were not reported and documented when they occurred.

Students should not contact the instructional team to request a make-up examination for a nonapproved reason. Such requests will not be approved.

Technical Incidents During an Assessment #

Students should not wait until the final hours of an assessment window to begin.

Before beginning an assessment, students are responsible for confirming that:

  • Their computer is powered and functioning correctly.
  • Their operating system and browser are supported.
  • The Honorlock extension is installed and working.
  • Their camera and microphone are functioning.
  • Their internet connection is stable.
  • External monitors are disconnected.
  • Unauthorized applications and browser tabs are closed.
  • Their testing environment satisfies the Honorlock requirements.

If a problem occurs before an attempt begins, the student should contact Honorlock Support and resolve the problem before starting whenever possible.

If an Honorlock or Canvas problem occurs after an attempt has begun, the student must:

  1. Contact Honorlock live support immediately.
  2. Follow the instructions provided by the support representative.
  3. Preserve the support case number and chat transcript, when available.
  4. Take screenshots of relevant error messages when doing so does not violate assessment rules.
  5. Notify the instructional team through a private Ed Discussion post as soon as the immediate technical issue has been addressed.
  6. Include the support case number, approximate time of the problem, and a concise description of what occurred.

The student should not include assessment questions, screenshots of assessment content, or sensitive personal information in the Ed Discussion post.

A technical problem does not automatically result in additional time, a new attempt, or a make-up assessment. The instructional team will review contemporaneous technical records and Canvas/Honorlock information before determining whether any action is appropriate.

A problem reported only after the assessment window closes, without contemporaneous Honorlock support documentation, may be impossible to verify.

Honorlock Circumvention and Unauthorized Technology #

Any attempt to bypass, disable, interfere with, deceive, or circumvent Honorlock or another assessment-security requirement is a serious suspected academic-integrity violation and will be referred to the Georgia Tech Office of Student Integrity.

Prohibited conduct includes, but is not limited to:

  • Using a virtual machine.
  • Using a remote desktop.
  • Using screen-sharing software.
  • Using a second operating-system session.
  • Using a secondary computer.
  • Using a phone or tablet.
  • Connecting an external monitor.
  • Using an unauthorized browser extension.
  • Accessing an unauthorized website or application.
  • Receiving assistance through another person or device.
  • Manipulating the camera, microphone, browser, or testing environment.
  • Attempting to obtain or use an assessment access code outside the authorized Honorlock process.
  • Using any technology or method intended to evade monitoring or obtain unauthorized assistance.

The use or attempted use of an unauthorized method is prohibited even if the method does not ultimately provide the student with an advantage.

Suspected unauthorized access, unauthorized collaboration, Honorlock circumvention, sharing of assessment content, or use of prohibited resources will be handled through Georgia Tech’s academic-integrity procedures.

Possible sanctions may include a zero on the affected assessment, failure in the course, disciplinary probation, suspension, or expulsion from Georgia Tech. Findings and sanctions are determined through the official Georgia Tech process.
